**Scope: placement + stackup + zones + keepouts + full net assignment +
readable silkscreen. No traces are routed** — placement is the
judgment-heavy part; routing a 6-block motor+RF+audio board blind would
produce something plausible-looking and wrong. The ratsnest is complete
and net-correct, ready for interactive routing.

## v3: why v2 was thrown away

v2 bin-packed each block's parts into rectangular zones. That guaranteed
clearance but placed nothing *functionally*: decoupling caps landed
wherever the packer put them, not at their IC pins. v3 replaces the packer
with an explicit per-part placement:

- **Every one of the 183 parts has a hand-chosen seed position derived
  from its connectivity** (in `PLACEMENT`, organised by block, with the
  reasoning in comments): decouplers at their supply pins, FB dividers at
  FB pins, converter magnetics at their SW nodes, gate resistors at their
  FETs, protection parts at the battery terminal they guard.
- A bounded **relaxation pass** (`relax()`) then enforces real assembly
  clearance (≥0.65 mm courtyard-to-courtyard, ≥0.85 mm at connectors,
  target incl. margin) by pushing colliding parts apart along the cheapest
  axis, capped at 4 mm (ICs/connectors) / 7 mm (passives) from the seed so
  functional adjacency survives. Constraint-pinned parts (`LOCKED`) never
  move.
- `qa_locality()` closes the loop: every 2/3-pad satellite must end up
  near another pad of its (non-rail) net — the report proves relaxation
  didn't undo the functional placement.

### Back-side mirroring (the fact v3 is built around)

`pcbnew`'s `Flip(LEFT_RIGHT)` mirrors X **and leaves the stored
orientation at 180°**. `add_footprint()` therefore applies B-side
rotations as absolute `(180+rot)%360`, giving the `PLACEMENT` table a
sane convention (rot=0 ⇒ pad(lx,ly) → world (ax−lx, ay+ly)). Consequence:
an IC's local-left pin column faces world **right** at rot 0 — e.g. U8's
USB/XTAL/3V3/EN pins face world right (so USB entry, crystal, decoupling
sit right of the module), U12's outputs face the motor at rot 0 while U11
needs rot 180. `verify_pins()` asserts 9 critical pad positions at build
time so a transform regression can't slip through silently.

## Stackup & rules

4-layer, 1.6 mm; In1/In2 = solid GND (RF + 2 stepper drivers + class-D amp
⇒ two clean return planes beat a hand-split power plane; the 6 rails are
short regional trunks on the outer layers). Min clearance **0.1 mm** (J1's
native 0.5 mm-pitch pads, U5 SOT-563) and min through-hole **0.2 mm** (the
stock ESP32 footprint's thermal-via-in-pad array) — both inside PCBWay's
advanced tier, flag when ordering. Default track 0.25 mm, via 0.6/0.3.

## Verification (all reproduced by re-running the build)

- build QA: **0 courtyard errors** (175 pairs in the 0.65–1.2 mm "tight"
  band — by design), **0 keepout/edge violations** (motor circle checked
  against its true centre = M1's anchor (55,61), not the shaft; ESP32
  keepout as exact rects; SD mouth corridor; 1 mm edge margin).
- `pcb_check.py` (independent): 0 motor-keepout, 0 board-edge; 1 F-side

  thermal-via array, and its pads-bbox spans the module; the only actual

- `kicad-cli pcb drc --severity-all`: **0 clearance, 0 shorts, 0 drill, 0
  courtyard** except: 483 unconnected (the ratsnest — nothing is routed),
  55 silk warnings (cosmetic, dense clusters), 1 courtyard overlap =
  F1↔BT1 (the intentional TCO-against-holder thermal contact).
- `qa_locality()`: 13 parts >10 mm from their signal net, each deliberate:

  the FET end of the wake PWM runs), R62/R63/R111/R110 (mid-route pulls /
  series parts), R26/R27/Q8/R93/R94 (intra-cluster hops).

## Schematic fix found by this pass (2026-07-23)

**U1 CH224K footprint was fab-fatal**: `b_powerin.py` overrode the stock
symbol's footprint with `TSSOP-10_3x3mm_P0.5mm`. The CH224K is **ESSOP-10**
(3.9×4.9 body, **1.0 mm** pitch) and its **only GND is the exposed belly
pad** (pin 11) — on the old footprint U1 had no ground connection at all
(caught as the single unmatched pad→net lookup) and the wrong lead pitch.
Now `Package_SO:SSOP-10-1EP_3.9x4.9mm_P1mm_EP2.1x3.3mm` (the symbol's own
default). ERC unchanged (2 known-benign). ⚠ cross-check the EP size vs the
WCH drawing before fab, and note the EP wants a few thermal vias so it can
be soldered from the front side with an iron (project hand-solder rule).

`pcb_build.py` re-derives BOM + netlist from `clock.kicad_sch` every run,
so schematic edits flow through on the next build; a new part just needs a
`P(ref, x, y, rot, side)` line in its block (the build refuses to run on a
placement/schematic mismatch).

## 3D models (2026-08-02)

Every part on the board now has a body in the 3D viewer: **182 of 186
footprints resolve a model file**, the four exceptions being the M3 mounting
holes (nothing to draw). `kicad-cli pcb export step` loads all of them
without a warning, and the exported assembly measures **Z −18.845 … +10.815**
— i.e. the motor sets both extremes: 18.76 mm of body behind the board and
10.9 mm of shaft in front of the mounting face (9.2 mm clear of the front
copper), which is the number the dial/enclosure work is measured against.

| Ref | Was | Now |
|---|---|---|
| M1 Juken X40 | body floating ~18 mm in **front** of the dial | `(offset 0 0 18.26)` — the old value had the right magnitude and the wrong sign; +18.26 puts the mounting face (model y = −18.26, where the snap pegs and both shafts start) flat on the back copper |
| U14 QRE1113GR | no model | vendor STEP, `(rotate 180 0 0)` (it is authored leads-up) + `(offset 0.8196 0.1417 5.5)` to put the seating plane on z = 0 and centre the package |
| J1 GCT USB4160 | no model | vendor STEP, `(rotate −90 0 0)` (authored Y-up) + `(offset 0 −1.31 0)`: the model's four shell stakes then land exactly on the footprint's ±4.00/±1.43 slots |
| BT1 Keystone 1043 | no model | vendor STEP, `(rotate −90 0 180)` — the extra 180° about Z is what puts the model's Ø2.4 index post in the footprint's index hole at (−36.13, −8) instead of diagonally opposite |
| J7/J10/J11 JST ZH | stock footprint pointed at a **file KiCad doesn't ship** | generated, see below |
| F1 Cantherm SDF TCO | no model | generated; it sits 1.2 mm from the 18650 holder, so it needs a body to be worth checking |

**Where the models come from.** Vendor STEPs are dropped into `3d/` as-is and
aligned by the footprint's `(offset)/(rotate)` (same convention as the
pre-existing `X40_879.step` / `FH34SRJ`). The two that no vendor supplies are
generated by `gen/mk3d.py` (pythonocc under FreeCAD's interpreter) straight in
KiCad's 3D frame, so they need neither offset nor rotation:

- `JST_ZH_B{2,6}B-ZR_…step` — KiCad 10 ships the ZH *footprints* but no ZH
  models at all (`Connector_JST.3dshapes` has EH/GH/PH/SH/XH and stops), so
  the stock footprint's `(model …)` line points at a nonexistent file. Built
  from `datasheet/connector_jst_zh.pdf` p.4.
- `Cantherm_SDF_TCO.step` — Ø4.0 × 10.5 body on the footprint's own 20.32 mm
  pitch with AWG18 axial leads.

Both carry per-solid colour: pythonocc's XCAF layer aborts in FreeCAD's build,
so `mk3d.py` appends the AP214 presentation entities to the plain writer's
output itself.

**The frame, once, because every sign in the table above depends on it:**
X = footprint X, **Y = −footprint Y**, Z up out of the mounting side (KiCad
mirrors it for B.Cu parts), offsets in mm applied *after* rotation, and each
`(rotate (xyz a b c))` value is applied as a **negative** rotation about that
axis, X first, then Y, then Z.

**Keeping a routed board in sync.** `pcb_build.py` regenerates the PCB from
scratch, which is not something you can do to this board any more. So
`gen/models3d.py` does double duty: `apply_override()` is called from
`add_footprint()` for future regenerations, and `sync_pcb()` patches only the
`(model …)` blocks of an existing `.kicad_pcb` in place, taking them from
`clock.pretty` (the source of truth) or from the `OVERRIDES` table, and leaving
every other byte alone. Re-run it after any footprint model edit:

```
python3 gen/models3d.py clock.kicad_pcb     # idempotent; prints what it changed
/Applications/FreeCAD.app/Contents/Resources/bin/python gen/mk3d.py   # if the generated models changed
```

### `OnSemi_QRE1113GR` pad pitch — found by the 3D pass, fixed 2026-08-02

The footprint read the datasheet's land-pattern **"2.59" as a centre-to-centre
pitch** and put the pads at y = ±1.295. It is an *overall* dimension, exactly
like the "5.66" in the same figure that the same footprint correctly turned
into x = ±2.00 centres: 2.59 = 2 × 0.79 pad + gap ⇒ centres at **y = ±0.90**,
which is the 1.80 mm lead pitch on the package drawing (98AON13407G). The
vendor STEP is what made it visible — its lead feet sat hard against the
inboard edge of each pad, overlapping only 0.25 mm of the 0.79 mm width.

Fixed in `clock.pretty/OnSemi_QRE1113GR.kicad_mod` (pads, the F.SilkS pin-1
dot and the F.Fab pin-1 leader all move to ±0.90) and applied to the routed
board with **`gen/reseat_pads.py`**, which exists for exactly this case: it
moves an instance's pads to their library positions, drags the track endpoints
that sat on the old pad centres along with them, and moves the footprint's own
graphics that marked a pad row — and nothing else. U14: 4 pads (±0.395 mm in
world X), 2 F.Cu track endpoints (`Net-(U14-A)`, `HOME_OPTO`), 3 graphic
points. Then `gen/pcb_fill.py`, then DRC: **0 violations, 0 unconnected**
(the pre-existing `lib_footprint_mismatch` warning on U14 is gone with it).

```
python3.9 gen/reseat_pads.py clock.kicad_pcb U14   # then always:
python3.9 gen/pcb_fill.py    clock.kicad_pcb       # moved copper ⇒ stale pours
```

Two traps both tools now handle, worth knowing before writing another:

- **`board.Save()` rewrites the sibling `.kicad_pro`** from pcbnew's own model,
  which can reset the design rules to defaults — after which `kicad-cli pcb
  drc` cheerfully reports ~500 phantom clearance violations against 0.2 mm.
  Everything that edits geometry only saves through `pcb_io.save_board()`,
  which puts the `.kicad_pro`/`.kicad_prl` back byte for byte.
- **`kicad-cli pcb drc` loads the project by the board's basename**, so DRC-ing
  a scratch copy called anything other than `clock.kicad_pcb` also silently
  checks against default rules.

### Closed 2026-08-07: J7/J10/J11 stay `B*B-ZR`, the 2.7 mm-tail version

On this 1.6 mm board the pins clear the far side by only ~0.9 mm (measured off
the STEP export); JST specifies the 2.7 mm tail for 0.6–1.2 mm boards and
`B*B-ZR-3.4` for 1.6 mm. Same footprint, so it would be a BOM-line change —
**but the -3.4 parts are not procurable in ones.** Verified on DigiKey
2026-08-07: `455-B6B-ZR-3.4-ND` and `455-B2B-ZR-3.4-ND` are Active but **not
stocked** (made to order, MOQ 2,000, 16-week lead), while `455-B6B-ZR-ND` is
$0.24 with 9,478 on the shelf at MOQ 1.

**Deviation accepted.** A 2.7 mm post through a 1.6 mm board still leaves
1.1 mm protruding, which is a normal fully-wetted through-hole joint
(IPC-A-610 asks for a visible, wetted lead, not a particular length), and the
wafer seats on the board either way. Recorded in `gen/parts_db.py` on all
three references. Same call on the sensor board's J1 — these are the two ends
of one harness. Revisit only if a production run ever justifies 2,000 pieces.
